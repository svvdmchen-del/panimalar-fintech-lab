The proposition

One screen. Twelve ways
to learn finance.

Most MBA finance courses end with a valuation spreadsheet built on a case that is three years old by the time it is graded. This terminal puts the current market session on the projector — the index, the option chain, the institutional flows — and labels every number with where it came from and how old it is.

It is built for a 40-student lab and a dim projector: dense, dark, readable from the back row, no login and no API keys. Students open a browser and immediately start asking the questions an analyst asks.

Under the hood

Every number is labelled.
That is the lesson.

A dashboard is only as credible as its provenance. Each response carries a status, a source, an as-of timestamp and an optional warning. When NSE is unreachable, the request falls through to the MCP server, then Yahoo Finance, then a deterministic simulator — so the class never stares at a spinner, and never mistakes a simulation for the exchange.

The same discipline applies to an analyst’s model: a number without a source and a date is an opinion.

// every market response
{
  "data": [ … ],
  "status": "live",
  "source": "nse:indices",
  "asOf": "2026-10-27T10:32:11+05:30",
  "warning": null
}
Never blank

Every capability ends in the simulator. A dropped feed changes the label, not the lesson plan.

Label the truth

LIVE, DELAYED or DEMO on every panel — plus the actual source and timestamp.

Cache once, serve forty

TTLs from 15 seconds to 5 minutes; hidden tabs drop to one request per minute.

LIVE

Straight from the exchange endpoint. Freshest state.

DELAYED

Yahoo or MCP fallback, typically 15 minutes behind.

DEMO

Seeded simulation; the banner explains why it appeared.

Why an MBA should care

Analysts spend more time validating data than modelling. Asking “where did this number come from, and how old is it?” is a professional habit — and this terminal makes that habit visible on every card.

What to look at, and why

1
The tape is relative

Students compare % change, not price. A ₹12,000 stock and a ₹400 stock are only comparable as percentages.

2
Six windows, one market

Large cap vs mid cap vs IT vs volatility. Rotation begins as a relative number on a card.

3
Sparklines show character

Direction, slope and volatility in one glance. VIX falling while the index rises is the classic risk-on signature.

4
Breadth beats the headline

An index can rise while most constituents fall. The advance/decline rail exposes a narrow rally.

5
Movers separate news from noise

Gainers and losers show reaction; volume toppers show conviction. Ask which move has volume behind it.

6
Session status is context

Pre-open, live and post-close change how a quote should be read. Every panel carries its own clock.

A rising index, a falling majority

Breadth divergence: a handful of heavyweights can lift the index while most stocks decline. Ask: how many stocks are advancing?

One sector in disguise

Concentration: a “broad” index can be a single sector story. Ask: which sector drove the move?

A one-day move is not a trend

Timeframe: the same +2% is noise on a 1Y chart and a breakout on a 1D chart. Ask: over what horizon?

Structure before prediction

Higher lows from mid-session, then a break above the prior swing high. Students describe what happened before forecasting.

Volume confirms the move

The breakout bar carries roughly 2.5× average volume. A price move without volume is a hypothesis, not evidence.

The y-axis is a choice

Switch from 1D area to 1Y candles and the same stock tells a different story. Timeframe is an analytical decision.

Three traps to teach

Open interest is not volume. A large OI can be old positioning that never trades today.

PCR is a ratio, not a signal. It needs price context, OI change and expiry to mean anything.

Implied volatility is the price of uncertainty. Rising IV makes every option more expensive, including the hedge.

Known limitations — taught, not hidden

  • NSE has no official public API; some endpoints return 403 on campus networks, hence the MCP and Yahoo fallbacks.
  • Yahoo quotes are delayed by roughly 15 minutes — fine for teaching, not for trading.
  • FII/DII publishes only the latest session through the public endpoint.
  • Symbols change; unknown tickers render as “no quote” instead of disappearing.

Data ethics & attribution

Data belongs to NSE India, Yahoo Finance and the respective publishers. This is an educational, non-commercial classroom tool: not investment advice, not a trading system. Do not redistribute or resell the data; check NSE’s terms before exposing the dashboard outside the institution.
